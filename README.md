# Stellar (Unreal Engine 5.8)

A robotic industrial civilization on a huge, fully deformable planet: hand-built foundations
first, then interplanetary logistics and a stellar engine around a star or black hole.

This branch restarts the game in **Unreal Engine 5.8 / C++**, ported from the Godot 4.7 .NET
project (`fx46/StellarGodot`, `NewGodotProject`, commit `a0d7cc1`). It follows that project's
[development plan](Docs/DEVELOPMENT_PLAN.md) and its moodboard: **milestone 0, planetary scale,
comes first**. The 1000 km home planet, the astronaut and the sculpting tool are ported, and
milestone 0 is carried through: terrain forms and geology at several scales, atmosphere, an
editable and saveable big planet with a measured traversal trial (0), an industrial test site
with a planning view and structure placement (0b), and a terrain-coupled fluid spike (0c). See
[Docs/MILESTONE_0.md](Docs/MILESTONE_0.md) for the gates and their evidence. The ship factory,
drones and production wait for the milestones that rebuild them
(see [What is not ported yet](#what-is-not-ported-yet)).

## Run

- Unreal Engine **5.8** (installed from the Epic launcher) and Visual Studio 2022 with the
  "Game development with C++" workload.
- Right-click `Stellar.uproject` → *Generate Visual Studio project files*, or build directly:

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\Epic Games\UE_5.8\Engine\Build\BatchFiles\Build.bat" StellarEditor Win64 Development -Project="$PWD\Stellar.uproject" -WaitMutex
```

- Open `Stellar.uproject` and press Play, or run the game directly:

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\Epic Games\UE_5.8\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor.exe" "$PWD\Stellar.uproject" -game -windowed -ResX=1600 -ResY=900
```

The map (`/Game/Maps/Planet`) is empty on purpose: `AStellarWorld` builds the planet, sky
and light at runtime, and lays out the test site beside the start (`-notestsite` skips it).
The few assets (five materials and the map) are generated by
`Scripts/build_content.py`; rerun it after changing it:

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\Epic Games\UE_5.8\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe" "$PWD\Stellar.uproject" -run=pythonscript -script="$PWD\Scripts\build_content.py" -unattended -nosplash -nullrhi
```

### How the big planet works in Unreal

- **Coordinates.** Terrain and gameplay math run in the planet frame: doubles, metres, centre at
  the origin. `APlanetTerrainActor` sits at the planet's centre, rotated so the start site is at
  the top of the world with its ground at the world origin. Unreal's large world coordinates keep
  transforms in doubles and render camera-relative, which answers the plan's open
  "double precision versus rebasing" question without an engine rebuild. Each node's mesh is
  node-local (floats, centimetres), its density evaluated with the node origin in doubles.
- **Streaming.** A thread-pool job selects the octree cut for the camera and meshes only nodes
  that are new, whose coarser neighbours changed or that an edit reached; the game thread swaps
  the whole cut in one frame. Render resources are bounded by the cut; components are pooled.
- **Edits.** `FTerrainEdits` stores density offsets in 16³ bricks only where someone has dug.
  The density anywhere is procedural + trilinear offsets, so the first edit of a brick changes
  nothing anyone reads and every LOD sees the same terrain. Edits are immutable snapshots
  (meshing threads never see a half-applied bite) computed off the game thread, one at a time.
- **Collision.** The astronaut moves against the density field itself, as in Godot: no
  collision meshes are cooked for the thousand terrain components, nothing can fall through
  terrain that is still streaming, and fresh cuts are walkable immediately.
- **Sky.** A `SkyAtmosphere` whose planet is concentric with the terrain gives aerial
  perspective over tens of kilometres; the warm directional light is the accretion disk's.

## Tests

```powershell
& "C:\Program Files\Epic Games\UE_5.8\Engine\Binaries\Win64\UnrealEditor-Cmd.exe" "$PWD\Stellar.uproject" -ExecCmds="Automation RunTests Stellar; Quit" -unattended -nosplash -nullrhi
```

- `Stellar.Terrain.OctreeNeighbours`: at 60 and 1000 km, four camera heights, face neighbours are
  never two levels apart and no node is inside another.
- `Stellar.Terrain.HeightBounds`: sampled heights stay inside their interval bounds, reaches 1 m–50 km.
- `Stellar.Terrain.SparseEqualsDense`: sparse corner meshing equals dense meshing bit for bit, random transition faces.
- `Stellar.Terrain.ClosedMeshAndWinding`: a sphere meshes closed and manifold, every triangle facing out.
- `Stellar.Terrain.Edits`: brush offsets on the lattice, removed volume, build undoing dig, snapshot isolation, save round trip.
- `Stellar.Player.StandAndWalk`: landing settles a capsule radius up, standing still does not creep, walking keeps contact.
- `Stellar.Game.MiningCredit`: fractional credit and pocket capacity.
- `Stellar.Terrain.EditKinds`: graded pads are level, ramps drop at their slope, carved caverns are open under a roof, fill is recorded in the material channel, grading stores only near-surface bricks, old (version 1) bricks load.
- `Stellar.Terrain.PrecisionAt1000km`: node origin + float offset gives the same density as doubles, within 0.3 mm.
- `Stellar.Game.Structures`: IDs, overlap, footprint lookup, save round trip.
- `Stellar.Game.TestSite`: on the real start site, the yard is level, the ramp and pit at depth, the cavern open under rock, the pocket sealed, deposits iron in sandstone and copper in basalt.
- `Stellar.Fluid.Cellular` / `Stellar.Fluid.Heightfield`: the 0c spike's two representations (conservation, settling, zero cost at rest, breach, the heightfield's blind spot); they log the numbers in [Docs/FLUID_SPIKE.md](Docs/FLUID_SPIKE.md).

Two automated runs on a `-game` build back the milestone 0 gates (absolute folder paths):

- `-shots=<folder>`: the visual gate's compositions, from eye height out to 30 km, a landmark,
  the test site from the planning view, the ramp, both deposits, the cavern by lamplight, the
  spring and basin, and the aquifer breach (measured: the water in the pocket and the pit is
  logged before and after).
- `-trial=<folder>`: the technical gate. It fast-travels out to the far side of the planet,
  digs at every stop, and records streaming and edit latency, resident nodes, memory and frame
  percentiles. It checks footing, precision, revisits and save/clear/load, then writes
  `report.md`.

## What is not ported yet

Deliberately left for the milestones that rebuild them (development plan): the orbital factory
ship and its interior, machines, recipes, autocrafting and power (milestone 1 gives machines
local buffers and stable IDs, so `ShipSim`/`Crafting` are redesigned there rather than copied),
drones, hulls and the blueprint VM, the inventory/research/missions/codex/navigation screens,
nugget piles, the moving asteroid ring and detached fragments, the black-hole sky shader and
sounds. The Godot repository stays the reference for all of them. What milestone 0 still leaves
open is listed at the end of [Docs/MILESTONE_0.md](Docs/MILESTONE_0.md).
